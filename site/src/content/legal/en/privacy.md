# Privacy

**My Pets** is made by Binary Bridges. This page says what the app does with your information. Effective {{EFFECTIVE_DATE}}.

## The short version

- Your pets' records live on your devices and in your own iCloud. We can't read them.
- You don't create an account with us. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
- The only things we can read are the records behind a pet's collar tag link: the card you choose to publish on its public page, and the empty record that keeps the link yours (below).

## What stays with you

Everything you record about your pets (records, notes, photos, documents, reminders and settings) is stored on your iPhone or iPad. If iCloud is on, a copy is kept in your private iCloud database, encrypted so only your devices can read it. Photo copies are sealed on your device before they're uploaded. Apple stores this data under your iCloud account; we have no access to it.

When you share a pet with someone through iCloud, the people you invite can read that pet's record. When you pass a pet's record to a phone next to you, or export it to a file, it goes from your device straight to that phone or file. None of this reaches us.

The app uses your location to centre the map when a pet is lost and to name the area it was lost in. To name the area, the point is sent to Apple Maps, which answers with the neighbourhood or town; we never receive it. It reads your photo library only to connect the photos you pick with your pets' records.

## What you choose to publish

When you make a link for a pet's collar tag, the app saves a record for it in My Pets' public CloudKit database, run by Apple, before the link appears on any tag, code or poster. That first record holds no pet data: only the tag's public key and a signed notice that nothing is published, so the link stays yours.

If you turn on the public page for a pet (for example when it's lost, or so a collar tag shows your contact details), the app saves a signed card in that record. Anyone who has the pet's tag or link can read it. You choose what it holds, from:

- your pet's name, species, breed, photo and microchip number;
- your phone number, and another contact's name and phone;
- your vet's name and phone;
- allergies and current medication;
- while the pet is lost: when it went missing, an approximate area (a neighbourhood or town, never your address or the exact spot), a description, your message, and where and when it was last seen.

Apple stores with each card the time it was saved and an identifier of the iCloud account that saved it, which is specific to My Pets. The app uses that identifier only to know which cards are yours. Someone who holds two of your pets' links could tell they were published by the same account.

Cards can't be searched or listed: they're read only through their link.

**How long it stays.** A card stays on the page until you unpublish it or deactivate the tag. Closing a lost case doesn't remove the card: it's published again without the lost details. Unpublishing or deactivating replaces the card and its photo with a signed notice that holds no pet data. The app never deletes the record itself: the tag's public key, that notice, its times and the account identifier stay in the public database, so nobody else can take over the tag's link. A card that isn't updated for more than 395 days is no longer shown.

To have a record removed completely, write to us at [support@binarybridges.co](mailto:support@binarybridges.co) with the tag's link. Once it's removed, that link can't be used again safely: make a new link for the tag.

## The public page

When someone opens a pet's link at pets.binarybridges.co, the page asks Apple's CloudKit for that one card and shows it. The page has no analytics, no cookies and no other requests. Like any website, its host (Cloudflare) and Apple see the visitor's internet address and the link that was opened; we don't keep them. If a finder shares their location with you, the page puts it into a message the finder sends themselves; it never goes into a record.

## Beta versions

If you test a beta through TestFlight, Apple shares with us the crash reports and the feedback you choose to send. Beta versions may include an assistant that runs entirely on your iPhone; nothing it reads leaves your device.

## Apple

Apple runs iCloud, CloudKit and the App Store. What Apple collects, such as purchase records and the crash data you agree to share, is covered by Apple's privacy policy.

## Children

My Pets isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly collect information from them.

## Your choices

- Turn off iCloud for My Pets in Settings to keep everything on your device only.
- Unpublish a public card, or deactivate a tag, in the app; ask us to remove a tag's record completely (above).
- Export everything to a file, and delete the app and its iCloud data whenever you want (Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage Account Storage).
